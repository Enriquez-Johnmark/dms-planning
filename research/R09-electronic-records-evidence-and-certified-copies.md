**Question:** What may the Officetul Documents app truthfully claim about electronic copies, signatures, authenticity and "certified true copies"? Which features would strengthen evidentiary weight?
**Researched:** 2026-10-19. **Every source was accessed on 2026-10-19.** Claims cite `[S#]`; URLs are in section 10.
**Not legal advice.** This is research for a Philippine lawyer to confirm.

## 0. Bottom line (summary of sections 1–9)

| # | Finding | Label |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | A scan of paper is best treated as a **copy ("duplicate") of a paper original**, not an electronic original. The SC refused e-document status to photocopies with manual signatures (*Codilla*) and to fax printouts (*MCC*) [S6][S3]. | Verified fact (holdings); Estimate (scans) |
| B2 | Since 2020, a "duplicate" made by photography or "electronic re-recording" is admissible like the original, **unless** the original's authenticity is genuinely questioned or admission would be unfair (Rule 130 s.4(b)–(c)) [S5]. | Verified fact (rule); Estimate (scans covered) |
| B3 | "Scan and destroy" forces reliance on **secondary evidence**. The client must prove the paper was lost or destroyed **without bad faith**, after a diligent search (Rule 130 s.5; *Codilla*) [S5][S6]. | Verified fact |
| B4 | **E-signatures** are recognised, but equivalence to a handwritten signature must be **proved** (RA 8792 s.8; REE R6) [S1][S4]. | Verified fact |
| B5 | Legally recognised certified copies come only from a **public custodian** (public records) or a **notary's copy certification** (not for vital, public or publicly recordable records) [S5][S7]. A company's "CTC" is a private attestation. | Verified fact; Estimate (company attestation) |
| B6 | **eNotarization** went live **19 Oct 2026 (today)**. It does **not** include copy certification [S8][S11]. | Verified fact |
| B7 | **REE Rule 7** weight factors map directly onto hashing, audit logs, access control and documented procedures [S4]. | Verified fact; Recommendation |

## 1. RA 8792 (Electronic Commerce Act of 2000) and its IRR

Approved **14 June 2000** [S1].

| Topic | Section | What it says (paraphrase, short quotes) | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Definitions | s.5(c), (e), (f) | **Electronic data message:** information "generated, sent, received or stored by electronic, optical or similar means". **Electronic signature:** any distinctive mark, characteristic or sound in electronic form "representing the identity of a person", attached to or logically associated with the document, or any methodology adopted with the intention of authenticating or approving. **Electronic document:** information by which a right is established, an obligation extinguished or a fact proved, which is "received, recorded, transmitted, stored, processed, retrieved or produced electronically". | Verified fact [S1] |
| Data messages | s.6 | Information is not denied legal effect "solely on the grounds that it is in the data message". | Verified fact [S1] |
| Electronic documents and writing | s.7(a) | Electronic documents have the legal effect of any other document. A writing requirement is met if the document "maintains its integrity and reliability and can be authenticated". Integrity means it remained "complete and unaltered", apart from endorsements and normal changes in communication, storage and display. | Verified fact [S1] |
| Original form | s.7(c) **and** s.10 | **Both** sections deal with originals. s.7(c) (documents): there must be "a reliable assurance as to the integrity of the document from the time when it was first generated in its final form", and it must be displayable. s.10 (data messages and documents): integrity from first generation "in its final form" is shown "by evidence aliunde or otherwise". **The s.7 proviso:** the Act does not "vary any and all requirements of existing laws on formalities required in the execution of documents for their validity". | Verified fact [S1] |
| Electronic signatures | s.8 | An e-signature equals a handwritten one if it is proved that "a prescribed procedure, not alterable by the parties interested" existed under which: (a) the signer is identified and their access shown; (b) the method is reliable and appropriate; (c) signing was necessary to proceed; (d) the other party could verify the signature. | Verified fact [S1] |
| Presumptions | s.9 | It is presumed that the e-signature is the signature of the person it "correlates" to, and was affixed with the intention of signing. This does not apply if the relying party knew of defects, or if reliance was unreasonable. | Verified fact [S1] |
| Authentication | s.11 | This applies until the SC issues rules (it did, in 2001). An e-document is authenticated by proof of a security procedure for "detecting error or alteration". **System integrity** may be shown by evidence that the system was "operating in a manner that did not affect the integrity" of the document, or that the record was kept by an adverse party or by a non-party in the ordinary course. The SC may adopt "electronic notarization systems". | Verified fact [S1] |
| Admissibility and weight | s.12 | An e-document cannot be excluded solely because it is electronic. One meeting s.6 or s.7 "shall be the best evidence" of the transaction. **Weight** depends on the reliability of how it was "generated, stored or communicated" and how the originator was identified. | Verified fact [S1] |
| Retention | s.13 | A legal duty to keep documents "in their original form" is met by an electronic record that: (i) "Remains accessible so as to be usable for subsequent reference"; (ii) is kept in the format generated, sent or received, or in one that "can be demonstrated to accurately represent" it; and (iii) identifies the originator, addressee and date and time. **A third-party service may be used** (s.13(b)). | Verified fact [S1] |
| Other | s.14–15; s.5(j) | Admissibility and the presumption may be proved by affidavit, subject to cross-examination (s.14–15). A storage service provider has "no authority to modify or alter the content" (s.5(j)). | Verified fact [S1] |
| **IRR** | IRR s.9, 12, 13, 14, 20 | The IRR was issued under s.34 by DTI, DBM and BSP in **July 2000** (*MCC* gives 13 July [S3]). Contents: e-signature equivalence (s.13); presumption (s.14); retention (s.20); electronic means **not mandatory** (s.9); **"Solemn Contracts"**, where a mandatory form stays "absolute and indispensable" (s.12). *MCC* held that the IRR overreached by adding fax to "data message". | Verified fact [S2][S3]; JAO number **Open question** |

**Estimate:** for **born-digital** files (emailed PDFs, e-invoices, system exports), s.13 is the hook for "electronic retention satisfies a duty to keep originals". For **scans of paper**, s.13 is weaker. The scan is not "the format in which it was generated". The client would have to show it "accurately represent[s]" the original, and *Codilla* and *MCC* suggest courts treat paper-derived images as paper copies (see §3). **Open question** for a lawyer.

## 2. Rules on Electronic Evidence (A.M. No. 01-7-01-SC) and the 2019 amendments

The Rules took effect **1 Aug 2001** (Rule 12 s.2) [S4].

| Rule | Content | Label |
|---|---|---|
| R1, R2 | Covers civil, quasi-judicial and administrative cases. Extended to criminal cases in 2002 (as cited in *People v. Enojas*). An **electronic document** includes "digitally signed documents and any printout… which accurately reflects" the electronic data (R2 s.1(h)). Digital and electronic signatures are defined in R2 s.1(e) and (j). | Verified fact [S4][S6]; criminal extension **Secondary** [S23] |
| R3 s.1–2 | **Functional equivalent** of a writing. Admissible if it complies with the Rules of Court and is **authenticated** under these Rules. | Verified fact [S4] |
| R4 s.1 | **Original:** an e-document is an original if it is a printout or output "shown to reflect the data accurately". | Verified fact [S4] |
| R4 s.2 | **Copies:** copies made at the same time or by equivalent accurate techniques are originals, **unless** "a genuine question is raised as to the authenticity of the original" or admitting the copy would be unjust. | Verified fact [S4] |
| R5 s.1–2 | The proponent bears the burden. A **private** e-document offered as authentic needs: (a) a digital signature by the purported signer; (b) "other appropriate security procedures or devices as may be authorized by the Supreme Court or by law"; or (c) "other evidence showing its integrity and reliability to the satisfaction of the judge". | Verified fact [S4] |
| R5 s.3 | An **electronically notarized** document is a **public document**, proved as a notarial document. | Verified fact [S4] |
| R6 s.1–4 | An authenticated e-signature equals a handwritten one. **Authentication** is by: (a) a method to create and verify a digital signature; (b) other means provided by law; or (c) "any other means satisfactory to the judge". **Presumptions:** the signature belongs to the person and was affixed with intent, and the methods "operated without error or fault". **Digital signatures add:** the certificate information is correct, the certificate was valid when signed, and the message has not been altered "from the time it was signed". | Verified fact [S4] |
| R7 s.1 | **Weight factors:** (a) reliability of how the document was "generated, stored or communicated", including "input and output procedures, controls, tests and checks for accuracy and reliability"; (b) reliability of identifying the originator; (c) **integrity of the information and communication system**, including hardware, software and programming errors; (d) the witness's familiarity with the system; (e) the nature and quality of the information that went into the system; (f) other accuracy or integrity factors. | Verified fact [S4] |
| R7 s.2 | **System integrity** may be shown by: (a) evidence the system operated without affecting integrity; (b) recording or storage by an **adverse** party; or (c) recording or storage "in the usual and ordinary course of business" by a **non-party** not under the proponent's control. | Verified fact [S4] |
| R8 s.1–2 | **Business records exception** to hearsay. Covers records made by electronic or similar means "at or near the time" by a person with knowledge, in the regular course of business, where making them was regular practice. Shown by the custodian or a qualified witness. Rebuttable by showing "untrustworthiness of the source" or of the method of preparation, transmission or **storage**. | Verified fact [S4] |
| R9 s.1–2 | Admissibility and weight may be proved by **affidavit** based on personal knowledge or authentic records. The affiant must be competent, affirms the affidavit in court, and may be cross-examined. | Verified fact [S4] |

### 2019 Amendments to the Revised Rules on Evidence (A.M. No. 19-08-15-SC)

Approved 8 Oct 2019; effective **1 May 2020** [S5].

| Provision | Change | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Rule 130 s.2 | Documents include writings, recordings and photographs. **"Photographs include still pictures, drawings, stored images…"** | Verified fact [S5] |
| Rule 130 s.3 | The "Best Evidence Rule" is renamed the **"Original Document Rule"**. Exceptions to producing the original: (a) lost, destroyed or cannot be produced, without the offeror's bad faith; (b) in the adverse party's custody; (c) numerous accounts; (d) a **public record** in a public officer's custody; (e) "not closely-related to a controlling issue" (new). | Verified fact [S5] |
| Rule 130 s.4(a) | An original is the document or any counterpart "intended to have the same effect" by the person executing it. For computer data, any printout "shown to reflect the data accurately" is an original. This **imports REE R4 s.1 into the general rules**. | Verified fact [S5] |
| Rule 130 s.4(b)–(c) | **New "duplicate" concept:** a counterpart produced "by means of photography… or by mechanical or electronic re-recording… or by other equivalent techniques which accurately reproduce the original". It is **admissible to the same extent as an original** unless there is a genuine authenticity question or it would be unjust. This mirrors REE R4 s.2. | Verified fact [S5] |
| Rule 130 s.5–8 | Secondary evidence when the original is unavailable (copy, then recital, then testimony, in that order). Adverse party's custody. **Summaries** (new; originals must be available for examination). Public records proved by **certified copy from the custodian** (s.8). | Verified fact [S5] |
| Rule 130 s.45 | The business-records hearsay exception now expressly covers records made "by electronic, optical or other similar means". | Verified fact [S5] |
| Rule 132 s.19–30 | Public versus private documents. Private documents need proof of due execution and authenticity (s.20). Official records are proved by an **attested copy** from the legal custodian (s.24). The attestation states the copy is "a correct copy of the original", under official seal (s.25). Notarized instruments are admitted without further proof (s.30). | Verified fact [S5] |

**Interaction (Estimate):**
- The 2019 amendments did **not** repeal or restate the REE. The REE still governs authentication of e-documents (R5), e-signatures (R6) and weight (R7).
- What changed is that the **general** rules now share the REE's definition of an original printout and its **duplicate** doctrine, and the duplicate doctrine now applies to **paper-derived copies** too.
- Before 2020, *Codilla* treated photocopies as inadmissible unless a secondary-evidence foundation was laid [S6]. After 2020, an accurate reproduction is presumptively admissible as a "duplicate" unless authenticity is genuinely disputed.
- **Open question:** whether courts have applied s.4(b) to PDF scans. No post-2020 SC case was located in this pass.

## 3. Is a scan an "electronic document" or a copy?

| Source | Holding or rule | Label |
|---|---|---|
| *NPC v. Codilla*, G.R. No. 170491, 4 Apr 2007 | Photocopies of manually signed letters are **not** electronic documents: "By no stretch of the imagination can a person's signature affixed manually be considered as information electronically received, recorded…". **Secondary-evidence predicates:** loss or destruction without bad faith, which "can be shown by circumstantial evidence of **routine practices of destruction** of documents"; and a "diligent and bona fide but unsuccessful search". | Verified fact [S6] |
| *MCC v. Ssangyong*, G.R. No. 170633, 17 Oct 2007 | A fax involves "scanning an original copy" and reprinting it. It is "paper-based", and e-documents "do not include a facsimile transmission". Photocopies of printouts are "paper records subject to the usual rules about copies". | Verified fact [S3] |
| SC eNotarization FAQ | Scans of wet-signed papers "are considered electronic documents **for purposes of the Rules on eNotarization**". | Verified fact [S8] |
| Rule 130 s.4(b) (2019) | Reproduction by photography, "electronic re-recording" or "equivalent techniques" is a **duplicate**. | Verified fact [S5] |

**Estimate (needs lawyer confirmation):**
- **Classification.** A scan of a paper document most likely sits as a **duplicate of a paper original** under Rule 130. It is not an electronic original under REE R4 s.1. *MCC*'s reasoning on faxes (scan, transmit, reproduce) applies closely to a scanner. The eNotarization FAQ's contrary wording is expressly limited to eNotarization.
- **Undisputed authenticity.** If authenticity is not genuinely disputed, the scan is admissible like the original (s.4(c)). Its **weight** then depends on proof that it accurately reproduces the original. Capture metadata, the hash and the custodian's affidavit help here.
- **Disputed authenticity.** If authenticity is disputed (typical for a contested signature), the court will want the **paper original**. Without it, the client must lay the s.5 foundation (no bad faith, diligent search). A hash proves the **scan** has not changed since intake. It proves nothing about whether the paper was genuine.
- **Public records.** Titles, SEC certificates, court orders and similar records can be proved by a **certified copy from the custodian** (s.3(d), s.8). A client who destroys their copy of a public record loses little legally, though they lose practical convenience.

**"Scan and keep" vs "scan and destroy":**

| Practice | Evidentiary position | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Scan and keep the paper (Officetul's policy) | The original can be produced if authenticity is challenged. The scan serves as a duplicate or working copy. The app can record the original's location. | Estimate |
| Scan and destroy under a written, routine, pre-dispute policy | The client may lay a secondary-evidence foundation (*Codilla*'s "routine practices"), but carries the burden. Exposed whenever authenticity of the original (especially signatures) is disputed. | Estimate |
| Destroy after a dispute is foreseeable | Risks a **bad-faith** finding, which blocks secondary evidence (s.3(a), s.5). | Estimate |
| Instruments that must be in a **public document** (§4), notarial registers, and BIR "hard copy" items | Paper (or a properly e-notarized original) remains the legal instrument. A scan does not replace it. BIR items are in **R07** §1. | Estimate; Open question |

## 4. Electronic vs digital signatures, PNPKI, wet ink and notarization

### 4.1 What counts

| Point | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|
| E-signature (broad) | Any mark, characteristic or sound, or methodology, representing identity with intent to sign. Typed names, drawn signatures, click-to-sign and **scanned signature images** all fall within the definition. | Verified fact (definition) [S1][S4]; Estimate (examples) |
| Equivalence to handwritten | Must be **proved**: a "prescribed procedure, not alterable by the parties" that identifies the signer, is reliable, is necessary to proceed, and is verifiable by the other party (RA 8792 s.8). Or it is authenticated under REE R6 s.2, including "any other means satisfactory to the judge". | Verified fact [S1][S4] |
| Digital signature | Asymmetric cryptography. Extra presumptions apply, including that the message was not altered after signing (REE R6 s.4). | Verified fact [S4] |
| A scanned image of a wet signature | *Codilla*: a manually affixed signature is not electronically produced information. A **picture of a wet signature** on a scan is evidence of the paper signature, not an e-signature. | Verified fact [S6]; Estimate (application) |

### 4.2 PNPKI (DICT)

| Point | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | **EO 810** (15 Jun 2009) institutionalised a "National Certification Scheme for Digital Signatures". Structure: Root CA and Government CA (originally NCC); RAs; DTI-PAO accredits government **and private** CAs (s.3). | Verified fact [S13] |
| Who may be issued certificates | The Government CA issues certificates "to government employees/entities and specific purpose certificates to private individuals/entities" (EO 810 s.3(b)). | Verified fact [S13] |
| Government use | Agencies providing e-services "shall require the use of digital signatures" (s.4). DTI is to *promote* digital signatures in the private sector (s.6). This is not a mandate on private firms. | Verified fact [S13] |
| DICT took over | RA 10844 s.15 moved NCC's functions to DICT. **DICT Department Circular 2017-001** (18 Jan 2017) approved PNPKI Certificate Policy v2.0 and also covers "private entities that will make use of the services of the Philippine RootCA". | Verified fact [S14] |

### 4.4 2004 Notarial Rules and eNotarization

| Point | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Paper notarization | The signatory must be personally present and known or identified (2004 Rules, Rule IV s.2(b)). | Verified fact (reproduction) [S7] |
| eNotarization scope | PDF and PDF/A only, done in person (IEN) or remotely (REN). Notarial wills and depositions are excluded. Wet-signed paper stays under the 2004 Rules. | Verified fact [S8] |
| Electronic notarial acts | Acknowledgment, affirmation or oath, jurat and signature witnessing (Rule IV ss.1–4, per the SC FAQ). **No electronic copy certification.** | Verified fact [S8] |
| Signatures and facilities | ENPs must use a "Digital Signature" or a "Secure Electronic Signature", through an SC-accredited facility (ENF) with e-KYC, MFA and liveness detection. | Verified fact [S8] |
| Legal effect | Same "validity, force, and effect" as a 2004-Rules notarial act, and a public document under REE R5 s.3. | Verified fact [S8][S4] |
| **Verification** | The SC Document Verification Portal lets anyone "verify the authenticity of the printed notarized document" through the issuing ENF. The seal carries a barcode or QR code. Records flow to the **SC Central Notarial Database**. | Verified fact [S8][S10]; QR **Secondary** [S12] |
| Dates | Promulgated 4 Feb 2025; uploaded to the SC site 9 Mar 2025. Nationwide rollout **19 Oct 2026**. | Verified fact [S9][S11]; exact effectivity **Open question** |

**Estimate:**
- An **e-notarized PDF** is the one artefact a DMS can hold where the **file itself** is a public document, not a copy.
- If the app stores the ENF-issued PDF unaltered (hash at intake) and links to the SC portal for verification, it preserves that status.
- Any re-rendering would break the signature and the seal. Examples: re-saving, OCR-flattening, PDF/A conversion, watermarking.

## 5. Certified true copies (CTC)

| Who | Basis | Scope and limits | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public officer with legal custody** | Rule 130 s.3(d), s.8; Rule 132 s.24, s.25, s.27 | Official records are proved by a copy "attested by the officer having the legal custody". The attestation states it is "a correct copy of the original", under official seal. The same applies to authorised public records of private documents (s.27). | Verified fact [S5] |
| **Notary: copy certification** | 2004 Rules, Rule II s.4; Rule IV s.1(a)(5) | A notarial act in which the notary is presented with a document "that is neither a vital record, a public record, nor publicly recordable"; copies it or supervises the copying; compares the two; and determines the copy is "accurate and complete". It must be entered in the notarial register (Rule VI s.2(a)). **Not available for** birth or marriage certificates, court and agency records, or registrable deeds. | Verified fact (reproduction) [S7] |
| Notary: CTC of their own notarial record | Rule VI s.6 | The notary supplies a "certified true copy of the notarial record" on request and payment. | Verified fact (reproduction) [S7] |
| Electronic notary | eNotarization FAQ | Copy certification is **not** among the electronic notarial acts. | Verified fact [S8]; **Open question** whether the Rules themselves say otherwise |
| **Corporate secretary** | RCC (RA 11232) s.24, s.73; s.123 for an OPC | The secretary is a mandatory officer (citizen and resident). Corporations must keep records, which are inspectable "regardless of the form in which they are stored". The RCC requires certification "under oath by the corporate secretary" for **specific filings**, such as amended articles and by-laws. A "Secretary's Certificate" of resolutions is **market practice**; third parties rely on it when regular on its face. | Verified fact (RCC) [S19]; Secondary (practice) [S22] |
| **Any private company** | No statute found | A company may attest that a copy matches its own record. The attestation is a **private document**: it must be authenticated (Rule 132 s.20) and carries only the certifier's credibility. It is **not** a public-document CTC. | Estimate; **Open question** |
| Criminal exposure | RPC art. 171(7), art. 172; RA 10175 s.4(b)(1) | Falsification includes "issuing in authenticated form a document purporting to be a copy of an original document when no such original exists". This is public officers' falsification, extended to private individuals for public or commercial documents, and to private documents where there is damage. Computer-related forgery covers altering data "with the intent that it be considered… as if it were authentic". | Verified fact [S20][S21] |

**What would an "organisation-certified copy" feature mean? (Estimate)**
- In law, it is a statement by a named officer of the tenant that the output matches the record the tenant holds. Its value is as **business-record and authentication support** (REE R8, R9; Rule 130 s.45). It is not a CTC in the Rule 132 sense.
- Calling it a "Certified True Copy" invites confusion with public-officer and notarial CTCs.
- Applying it to **public records** (titles, government certificates) or **vital records** could mislead recipients. Notaries cannot copy-certify those either.
- **Safe framing (Recommendation):**
  - "**Copy issued from [Tenant] records**: [officer name, position] attests that this is a complete copy of the file held in [Tenant]'s document system as of [timestamp]. SHA-256: …. This is not a notarial or government certification."
  - Never print "Certified True Copy" automatically.
  - Let a **human officer** sign the attestation, with their name and role recorded in the audit log.

## 6. Mapping Rule 7 weight factors to app features (Recommendation)

| Factor (REE / RA 8792) | Feature |
|---|---|
| R7 s.1(a): how the document was **generated** (for scans, captured) | Capture record: scanner ID, settings, capture user and time, source ("paper original at [location]", "email from X", "upload"). This supports "accurately reproduce" (Rule 130 s.4(b)). |
| R7 s.1(a) **storage**; RA 8792 s.7(c), s.10 "integrity… from the time when it was first generated in its final form" | **SHA-256 at intake**, stored apart from the file. Original kept byte-for-byte. OCR text and renditions kept as **derived objects**. Scheduled re-hash ("fixity check") with logged results. |
| R7 s.1(a) **communication** | Hash printed on every export or share. Export manifest (file, hash, time, user). |
| R7 s.1(b) **originator** | Uploader identity via SSO or MFA. Email headers kept for emailed files. In-app sign-off method recorded (RA 8792 s.8(a)). |
| R7 s.1(c), s.2(a) **system integrity** | **Append-only, hash-chained activity log.** Role-based access. No hard delete of originals; legal hold. Documented change control and incident log. |
| R7 s.1(c) **time**; RA 8792 s.13(a)(iii) | NTP-synced server time. Optional RFC 3161 timestamp on intake hashes. A PH-accredited timestamp authority was not identified (**Open question**). |
| R7 s.1(d) witness familiarity; R9 affidavit | Per-tenant **system description** plus a **draft Rule 9 custodian affidavit**. |
| R7 s.1(e) input quality | Image-quality warnings (skew, blur, missing pages). Required "original location" field for paper. |
| R8 / Rule 130 s.45 business records | Capture at or near the time of the event. Retention schedules. |
| "Official version" marker | Log who marked it, when and why. Keep superseded versions. This shows which counterpart was "intended to have the same effect" (Rule 130 s.4(a)). |
| E-notarized or digitally signed PDFs | Detect them and store **unaltered**. Add a link to the SC Document Verification Portal. Block OCR and PDF/A rewriting of these files. |

**Caveat (Estimate):** a hash proves a file is unchanged **since the hash was taken**. It does not prove the paper was genuine or that the scan was complete. Marketing must not imply more.

## 7. Safe and unsafe claims

| Claim | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Digital copy for reference" | **Safe** | Accurate. Consistent with keeping paper. |
| "Integrity-checked" / "SHA-256 fingerprint recorded at upload" | **Safe** | Factual, if fixity checks actually run. |
| "Tamper-evident" | **Safe, with qualification** | True only if the hash and audit log are protected separately, and only for changes after intake. Add "for changes after upload". |
| "Tamper-proof" | **Avoid** | No system can prevent alteration of the paper or by privileged administrators. Overclaims. |
| "Full audit trail of who viewed, changed or shared" | **Safe** | If complete and append-only. |
| "Electronic documents are legally recognised in the Philippines (RA 8792)" | **Safe, with qualification** | True (s.6–7), but formalities laws still apply (s.7 proviso). |
| "Legally valid copy" | **Avoid** | Validity is a property of the act or instrument, not the copy. Misleading for public-instrument cases (art. 1358). |
| "Court-admissible" / "admissible in evidence" | **Avoid** | Admissibility is decided by the court. A scan is a duplicate that can be challenged (Rule 130 s.4(c)). Alternative: "**designed to help you authenticate records** under the Rules on Electronic Evidence". |
| "Satisfies the legal duty to keep originals" | **Avoid** | RA 8792 s.13 conditions apply. Scans of paper are a weak fit. BIR hard-copy rules (R07). |
| "BIR-compliant" | **Avoid** | BIR requires hard copies for manual and loose-leaf books, among other rules (R07). Not verified for scans. At most say: "helps organise records you must keep for BIR". |
| "Certified true copy" (auto-stamped) | **Avoid** | Implies a public-officer or notarial CTC (Rule 132 s.24–25; Notarial Rules Rule II s.4). Falsification risk if misused (RPC art. 171(7) and 172). |
| "Organisation-attested copy, issued from [Tenant] records by [officer]" | **Needs qualification** | Acceptable with an explicit disclaimer that it is not a notarial or government certification. |
| "E-signature legally equivalent to handwritten" | **Needs qualification** | Only if the s.8 procedure is proved. Say: "**electronic signatures are recognised under RA 8792**; their weight depends on the signing method". |
| "Digitally signed with PNPKI" | **Safe, but only if true** | Only for files actually signed with a PNPKI certificate and verified (with a CRL/OCSP check). |
| "Paperless office" / "go paperless" | **Needs qualification** | Conflicts with the no-destruction policy. Use "less paper handling" or "find paper fast". |
| "Notarized" / "e-notarized" badge | **Safe, but only if true** | Only for files detected as ENF-issued and verifiable at the SC portal. Never for uploads the user labels themselves. |
| "Official version" | **Safe, with qualification** | Make clear it is the tenant's internal designation, not a legal status. |
| "Data Privacy Act compliant" | **Avoid** (out of scope here) | Compliance is the controller's obligation. See other research. |

## 8. Implications for the Documents app (Recommendation)

1. **Keep "never advise destroying originals" as product doctrine.** Show an "Original location" field for paper and a "Paper original retained?" flag. Make it required for document types tied to arts. 1358, 749 and 1874 instruments and to BIR hard-copy items.
2. **Treat the original file as immutable evidence.** Hash at intake. Store OCR text, thumbnails and PDF/A renditions as derived artefacts linked to the original's hash. Run scheduled fixity checks and log the results.
3. **Make the activity log hash-chained and append-only.** Export it with any evidentiary bundle.
4. **Offer an "Evidence pack" export:** original file, hash, capture metadata, audit-log extract, system description and a **draft REE Rule 9 custodian affidavit** for the tenant's lawyer to finalise. This targets R7, R8 and R9 directly.
5. **Rename the CTC idea** to "Copy issued from records", signed by a named officer, with a fixed disclaimer. Do not offer it for public or vital records. Point users to the issuing agency for certified copies of those.
6. **Detect and protect e-notarized and digitally signed PDFs.** Never rewrite them. Show signature status and an SC Document Verification Portal link.
7. **Signatures.** Treat any in-app sign-off (approval of the "official version") as an RA 8792 e-signature. Log identity, MFA and intent. Do not claim handwritten equivalence. PNPKI signing support is a later option, worth building only if tenants already hold certificates.
8. **Marketing copy** must follow the table in §7. Have a lawyer review the claim list before launch.

## 9. Questions for a Philippine lawyer

1. Under Rule 130 s.4(b) (2019), is a PDF scan of a paper document a "duplicate" admissible to the same extent as the original? Or do *Codilla* and *MCC* still treat it as secondary evidence needing an s.5 foundation?
2. Can a tenant rely on RA 8792 s.13 (retention "in the form of an electronic… document") to meet a legal duty to keep **paper-origin** documents in original form? Or does s.13 cover only born-electronic records?
3. If a tenant destroys paper after scanning under a written, routine retention policy, does that satisfy the "without bad faith" predicate (*Codilla*)? Does the answer change for documents with signatures that might later be disputed?
4. Is there any post-2020 SC or CA decision admitting or excluding scanned PDFs, OCR text or DMS printouts under Rule 130 s.4 or REE R4?
5. May a private corporation or sole proprietor lawfully issue a document titled "Certified True Copy" of its own records? What wording avoids falsification risk under RPC arts. 171(7) and 172?
6. Does a corporate secretary's certification of a copy of a company record need notarization to be relied on by courts or agencies (SEC, BIR, banks)? Is a secretary's electronic signature on such a certification acceptable?
7. Do the Rules on Electronic Notarization (A.M. No. 24-10-14-SC) themselves (not only the FAQ) exclude copy certification? Do they say anything about the status of an e-notarized PDF stored in a third-party DMS, or of its printout?
8. What do SC–LRA JMC No. 1 and SC–SEC JMC No. 1 (s.2026) require for e-notarized documents to be accepted? Do they require retrieval from the ENF or SC database rather than a copy held by the client?
9. Is an in-app approval click with MFA and an audit log enough to meet the RA 8792 s.8 "prescribed procedure, not alterable by the parties"? What is missing?
10. Which documents commonly handled by law, accounting and construction SMEs need a public instrument or wet-ink signature, beyond Civil Code arts. 1358, 749 and 1874 and PD 1529 s.112? Can these be validly executed through eNotarization instead?
11. What is the IRR's official designation (Joint Administrative Order number and exact date)? Is it still in force unamended?
12. Is any private certification authority currently accredited by DTI-PAO under EO 810? Are PNPKI certificates available to private companies (not just individuals) for document signing?
13. Do our disclaimers expose Officetul to liability if a tenant presents an "organisation-attested copy" as a CTC? Should the attestation feature be limited to officers with a recorded authority?
